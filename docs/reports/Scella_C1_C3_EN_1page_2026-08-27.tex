{\large\color{efegray}\itshape the threshold that seals, the register that answers}
\end{center}
\vspace{2pt}

\textbf{The problem.} A regulated organisation wants to use AI without letting its data leave, and
must later answer a regulator, an auditor or a court \emph{in the language of the text that binds
it} --- without handing over its systems and without anyone having to take the operator's word.
Scella ships this as \textbf{two products that share one receipt}: \textbf{the Threshold}, a
sovereign gateway that seals every call at the perimeter, and \textbf{the Register}, which turns
those seals into the evidence a specific regulation asks for.

\hd{The Threshold (C3): the sealed perimeter --- 20 crates}
\begin{tight}
\item \textbf{It runs where the data is.} \texttt{docker compose up} brings up a gateway that serves
a run and writes its receipt. No key is needed to \emph{catch} a truncation, and none is shipped:
the demonstration runs on a public test key and says so. {\color{efesolve}\textbf{Solves (1):}} the AI comes to the data, not the reverse.
\item \textbf{Three states, never a silent success.} Every capability is \emph{served},
\emph{refused with its cause named}, or \emph{degraded and said so}. A missing precondition stops the
boot with exit 78 and \emph{names} the variable it wanted; a provenance that cannot persist reports
\texttt{degraded} with a reason, it does not quietly return 200. {\color{efesolve}\textbf{Solves (4):}} you always know what the system did \emph{not} do.
\item \textbf{Truncation exposed by counting.} The chain's root and node count are published to an
append-only log \emph{before} the receipt is served. A truncated bundle re-proves \emph{perfectly} ---
a valid chain's prefix is a valid chain --- so re-proof alone cannot see it. The published claim can,
\textbf{with no key and no cryptography}: two numbers disagree. {\color{efesolve}\textbf{Solves (0):}} the one failure re-proof is blind to.
\item \textbf{The receipt survives a rollout.} Verified against a real PostgreSQL: the gateway is
killed, \emph{another process} takes the port, and the receipt re-proves --- authentic, root
matching, chain and content intact. {\color{efesolve}\textbf{Solves (3):}} an incident does not cost you the proof.
\item \textbf{One authority analyser, zero exemptions.} A single component decides which host the
HTTP client will \emph{really} contact. It was proven necessary: \texttt{127.0.0.1:8080@evil.com}
used to pass as loopback and cut TLS towards \texttt{evil.com}, on 875 spelling variants. {\color{efesolve}\textbf{Solves (1):}} the perimeter has one door, and one guard.
\end{tight}

\hd{The Register (C1): the answer a regulation asks for --- 8 crates}
\begin{tight}
\item \textbf{Four texts, by name.} EU AI Act (2024/1689, art.~12 record-keeping), GDPR (2016/679),
CSDDD (2024/1760), LkSG. The artefact is produced in the vocabulary of the text, not in the
vocabulary of the software. {\color{efesolve}\textbf{Solves (2):}} an answer the compliance officer can hand over as-is.
\item \textbf{Derived, never re-declared.} The evidence is \emph{derived from the sealed chain}, so
the register and the receipt cannot drift apart: there is no second source of truth to keep in
sync, and rewriting one breaks the re-proof of the other. {\color{efesolve}\textbf{Solves (0) \& (2):}} the compliance document \emph{is} the proof.
\item \textbf{Erasure without breaking the chain.} A GDPR deletion destroys a per-case key; not one
byte of the sealed graph moves, and re-proof still passes. An erased field reads \textbf{``destroyed
on \emph{date}''} --- never ``unknown''. Confusing the two would tell a regulator that a case he saw
never existed. {\color{efesolve}\textbf{Solves (2) \& (3):}} the right to erasure and the duty to keep records, together.
\item \textbf{Joined, not welded.} The Threshold reaches the Register through one feature, \emph{off
by default}, guarded by a manifest check. The two products ship together or apart. {\color{efesolve}\textbf{Solves (2):}} buy the perimeter first, add the register when the audit comes.
\end{tight}

\hd{One receipt binds them}
Both products write the \textbf{same sealed node}: an HMAC chain, per-tenant key, re-derivable by any
key-holder; tampering with one link makes the next fail. It is re-proved by a verifier whose
\textbf{declared dependency tree is empty} --- \textbf{zero} crates --- so a third party recompiles it
from source and checks your receipt on his own machine, offline. \textbf{Backup, restore and key
rotation} exist: a \emph{retired} generation still re-proves everything it sealed, a \emph{destroyed}
one reads ``destroyed on \emph{date}'', and a restore distinguishes fourteen distinct outcomes ---
truncated, re-sealed, wrong key, destroyed generation --- rather than one ``verification failed''.
\textbf{Exact guarantee:} tamper-evident to whoever holds the key; \emph{not} trustless --- the log
holder is the key holder, so the anchor removes \emph{retroactive} rewriting, not trust.
{\color{efesolve}\textbf{Solves (0):}} a third party re-derives the proof offline, without your systems.

\hd{Measured}
\begin{tight}
\item \textbf{Engineering.} 22 crates across both products, \textbf{2{,}539 green tests, 0 red}
(measured 2026-08-27), and \textbf{15 executable checks} (\texttt{scripts/*-check.sh}): every claim
above is re-runnable in one command.
\item \textbf{The single command.} \texttt{docker compose up} brings the gateway up, serves a run,
exports its bundle, truncates a copy and \textbf{shows the contradiction} against the published
anchor --- end-to-end, verified.
\item \textbf{The rollout.} Against a real PostgreSQL: gateway killed, another process on the same
port, receipt re-proved --- \texttt{verified $\cdot$ root\_matches $\cdot$ chain\_intact $\cdot$
content\_intact}.
\item \textbf{The perimeter.} One authority analyser with an \emph{empty} exemption list, proven
necessary against \textbf{875} spelling variants of a loopback bypass.
\item \textbf{The register.} Four named texts; evidence derived from the sealed chain rather than
re-declared; erasure that destroys a key without moving a byte of the graph, and reports a
\emph{date} rather than an absence.
\item \textbf{Conservation.} Backup, restore and key rotation, with a \textbf{fourteen-way}
restore diagnosis and a retired generation that keeps re-proving everything it sealed.
\end{tight}
